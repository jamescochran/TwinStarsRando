Product audit and plan

Twin Stars Adventure Randomizer: from randomizer to the companion every Twin Stars player keeps open

A candid assessment of the app as it stands on the ux-audit branch, what the evidence says players need, and a phased, testable plan to build it. No code has been changed for this plan.

Prepared 2026-11-05 · Repo jamescochran/TwinStarsRando · Branch ux-audit (open as PR #111) · Live site serves main @ a1412cd

1Executive assessment

What it is. An unofficial, free, offline-capable web app for Button Shy's solo wallet card game Twin Stars: Adventure Series. It draws a character pair and a scenario from the packs you own, times the game, logs the result, tracks coverage of all combinations, runs the SYZYGY campaign modes, rolls dice, plays ambient radio and carries an offline rules FAQ.

Who it serves. A small, loyal, long-tail audience. BoardGameGeek lists about 1,358 owners of Series I and 772 of Series II. Close to half of the collection comments that say anything mention print-and-play copies. Players describe 15-minute sessions on a lunch table, in a car, at school pickup and on a lap tray. The game is no longer sold on Button Shy's own store, so new users are existing owners, print-and-play builders and second-hand buyers.

The candid verdict. The app has unusually good bones for a fan project: a fair randomizer (verified over 13,200 draws), strong escaping, a strict content policy, offline support and, after tonight's work, a coherent and accessible interface. It is also, as far as an extensive search could find, the only Twin Stars-aware digital tool in existence. But it solves the job players have least trouble with. Choosing a pair and scenario takes ten seconds with real cards. The jobs players actually struggle with sit around and inside the game, and the app barely touches them:

  • Understanding the rules at the table. 55 of 84 forum threads are rules questions. A reviewer wrote that having to look things up online undercuts a game whose whole point is portability.
  • Tracking game state without fiddly tokens. Players use paperclips, coloured cubes and sticky notes, and complain about tokens being bumped or blown away. The app has no tracker for a normal mission.
  • Studying character pairings. Engaged players call the pairing meta-game the real thrill of Twin Stars. The app already stores every result but offers no per-pair insight.

It also carries correctness and trust problems that a best-in-class product cannot have: SYZYGY ranks that contradict the rulebooks, two ways to silently lose play history, a truncated rulebook image, 31 MB of rulebook PNGs and a release process that shipped a build which failed CI.

5 / 10UX/UI before tonight. Solid core, but failing contrast, unreachable dialogs on phones, keyboard gaps.
7.5 / 10UX/UI on the ux-audit branch (two independent QA passes scored it 7.5 and 8).
6 / 10Product value today: useful, but misses the biggest at-the-table needs.
9+ / 10Achievable with Phases 0–3 of this plan, all buildable offline with no backend.

The biggest opportunities

  1. Become the table, not the dice cup. A token-free Mission Board for health, energy and scenario tracks, built for one hand and an unstable surface. This addresses the second-strongest pain in the evidence and nothing else on the market does it.
  2. Answer rules questions in context, offline. Merge the 54 new rulings already compiled tonight, fix the three answers that conflict with designer rulings, and show only the rulings for the two characters and scenario on the table.
  3. Turn the log into insight. Per-pair win rates, a pair-by-scenario heat map and a "Gauntlet" mode that takes one team through every scenario you own.
  4. Earn trust. Fix data loss, SYZYGY correctness and the release pipeline before adding anything, because a logbook nobody trusts is worse than no logbook.

5Feature concepts

Seven concepts tailored to how Twin Stars is actually played: roll two character dice, place them one at a time, check the scenario's combo icons, reduce one die and re-roll the other when there's no combo, spend energy on re-rolls, pass skill checks, and track health, energy and one to three scenario-specific tracks.

1. Mission Board: the table in your hand Evidence

What it does
When a mission starts, the mission card becomes a board. Large counters for each character's health (labelled Primary and Secondary), a shared energy pool preset from the chosen difficulty, and up to four named scenario tracks (for example “Base precision” and “Precision” drawn as visibly different markers). Every change is undoable. The screen stays awake. It survives a reload. Reaching zero health offers “Record Loss”; the win condition offers “Record Win” with the time pre-filled.
Why players want it
It replaces the paperclips, cubes and sticky notes players complain about, on exactly the cramped, wobbly surfaces they describe.
Why it's distinctive
No other tool does this; generic counter apps don't know Twin Stars' tracks or difficulty values.
Design notes
One-handed bottom zone for the most frequent controls. It must never automate decisions: players value choosing which die goes where. Safest data approach until Button Shy agrees: users enter starting values once per scenario and the app remembers them.

2. Rules Oracle: answers for the cards on your table Evidence

What it does
One offline search box over the FAQ and (with permission) the rulebook text. When a mission is loaded it opens pre-filtered to these two characters, this scenario and general rules. Each answer carries an authority badge (Designer ruling, Designer-approved, Community, Disputed) and a link to its source thread. A short “commonly missed” list per scenario surfaces the rulings players most often get wrong.
Why players want it
Rules confusion is the strongest pain in the evidence, and the review that named it said the online lookup is the problem.
Why it's distinctive
The official FAQ page covers three scenarios and one character; fan FAQs are scattered across forum threads and a wiki. This would be the single, offline, contextual reference.

3. Pairing Lab and the Gauntlet Evidence

What it does
From your own log: win rate per pair, a pair-by-scenario heat map, “pairs you've never beaten”, “your best team for this scenario”, and how Primary/Secondary order changes results. Gauntlet mode locks one pair and serves every owned scenario in turn with a progress strip, ending in a summary you can share as text.
Why players want it
Engaged players describe pairing as the game's “astonishingly deep meta-game”, and one already plays fixed teams through every scenario by hand.
Why it's distinctive
Generic loggers can't model pair, order, scenario, difficulty and droid together. The app already holds the data.
Lower-risk alternative to a combo calculator
Computing combo odds from card icon layouts would need a transcription of every card, which is core game data. Learning from the player's own results needs nothing from the cards.

4. Daily Mission Speculative

What it does
Every day, every player gets the same pair, scenario and difficulty, chosen deterministically from the date and limited to content in the core series. Players log the result and share a spoiler-free text card (“Twin Stars Daily · Nov 5 · Win · 14:02 · Hard”) to a Discord, a BGG thread or a group chat. A streak counter rewards consistency.
Why players want it
A shared moment in a solo game, and a reason to play today. Speculative: no player has asked for it.
Why it's distinctive
Works with no backend and no accounts, because the seed is the date.
Validate
Ship as a fake door (“Daily Mission, coming soon”) and measure taps before building.

5. Teach Mode and scenario setup cards Evidence

What it does
A first-run path: start with Escape the Brig, a 60-second turn walkthrough, links to the free official print-and-play starters and rule PDFs. Before any scenario, a setup checklist (dice needed, markers and where they start, whether Primary/Secondary matters here). An optional turn-step rail beside the dice roller reminds players to check combos, reduce before re-rolling, and that reducing below 1 forces the ⚠⚠ action.
Why players want it
“New player tips?”, setup-video requests and forgotten skill checks are all in the forums.
Design notes
Dismissible, never blocking. Some players dislike being managed, so it ships behind a toggle.

6. Campaign Map for SYZYGY Verified need

What it does
The rulebook layouts drawn as an interactive map: Mode I's 1-2-2-1 sections and Mode II's pyramid with win (black) and loss (red) paths. Your three characters' health, the bench choice, carry-over and eliminations are tracked automatically; the rank is computed exactly as the rulebook says.
Why players want it
SYZYGY needs heavy bookkeeping. Demand is uncertain (five logged plays on BGG), so correctness comes first and the map follows only if usage justifies it.

7. Collection Shelf Evidence

What it does
A visual inventory of everything published in the series, with how each item was released (wallet, Kickstarter stretch goal, ShyCon exclusive, free print-and-play), what you own down to the single card, and what you are missing. Ownership drives the randomizer, stats and coverage.
Why players want it
Collectors hunt for droid cards and the ShyCon-only Scenario 13; print-and-play owners have unusual subsets.
Note
Link only to official sources. A wishlist signal could even help Button Shy judge reprint demand, if they want it.

6The future experience

Design direction

Keep the identity players already know: the deep-space palette, Orbitron headings and the star-orange and teal accents. Push it toward a cockpit instrument: dense, glanceable, tactile. Big numerals for health and energy, physical-feeling counters with a short tick on change (respecting reduced motion), and a quiet chrome that disappears during play. Phone-first and one-handed, because the evidence says play happens in cars and on lap trays.

Core journeys

  1. Returning player, 15 minutes at lunch. Open the installed app offline, tap Draw, see setup notes, tap Start. Play with the board. On a rules doubt, tap the scenario name and read the filtered ruling. Win; the result and time are pre-filled; one tap saves.
  2. New print-and-play owner. Choose “I have the free starter” on the Shelf, follow Teach Mode through Escape the Brig, and finish a first mission without opening a PDF.
  3. Pairing enthusiast. Open the Pairing Lab, see that a favourite pair has never beaten Confine the Quarks, start a Gauntlet with that pair, and share the summary in the community.

7Roadmap

Phase 0: Ship and stabilize (now, about a week)

Merge PR #111 after its CI is green. Then, in parallel: release pipeline (R2), rulebook images (R3), data safety (R1), privacy-safe analytics events and notice (R9). These unblock everything else.

Phase 1: Correct and complete (one to two weeks)

FAQ merge and Rules Oracle v1 (R4), SYZYGY to the rulebook once approved (R5), collection by item (R7), fonts and loading (R10), install and share (R11). R4, R7, R10 and R11 can run in parallel; R5 waits for the owner's decision.

Phase 2: The defining features (two to four weeks)

Modularize the code first (R15), then Mission Board (R6) and Pairing Lab with Gauntlet (R8) in parallel. The new five-section navigation lands with the Mission Board.

Phase 3: Habit and onboarding (validated first)

Teach Mode and setup cards (R13), Campaign Map, Daily Mission (R14) and Collection Shelf, each gated on the fake-door or usage data from Phase 0's events.

Phase 4: Research-gated

Custom domain (R12), optional cross-device sync, export to BG Stats or BGG play logging, rules as accessible HTML text, and any card-data features. Each needs either permission from Button Shy or evidence of demand.
